## Patterns observed (→ rule candidates)

1. **No naked claims.** Every strong claim carries a number, timeframe, or study population ("50% after 28 days", "93% of subjects", "in 2 weeks"). "Clinically proven" IS used — but always attached to a specific measured result, never free-floating.
2. **Mechanism over magic.** Copy explains *how* it works ("dissolving dead skin cells and sebum from inner walls of pores"), not miracle outcomes.
3. **Hedge verbs.** "helps", "reduces", "reduces the appearance of" — never "cures", "eliminates", "removes acne permanently".
4. **Concentration always stated with ingredient.** "2% salicylic acid", "10% Niacinamide" — the % is part of the product's name itself.
5. **Provenance named.** Suppliers cited (Lonza/Switzerland, Merck/Germany) — transparency extends to sourcing.
6. **Safety is explicit, not fine print.** Age suitability, pregnancy guidance, patch-test note appear on every page.
7. **Softness exists but stays literal.** "baby-soft" is about texture, not a transformation promise. The voice is clinical, not cold.
